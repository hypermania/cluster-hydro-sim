\section{Source and discrete equations}
All quantities below are dimensionless in the existing HydroSim units.
The POWER\_LAW number source and mass-transfer rate are
\begin{equation}
 S=A\rho_s^2u_s^{-3/5},\qquad \dot\rho_b=-\dot\rho_s=m_b S,
 \qquad m_b=2m_s.
\end{equation}
The external Statler initializer retains the Maxwellian average of the
Press--Teukolsky cross section used by the hydrostatic reproduction:
\begin{equation}
 A=\frac{107\Gamma(0.9)}{50\,2^{0.7}3^{0.4}\pi^{3/2}}
 \frac{(R_*/r_0)^{0.9}m_s^{-1.9}}{\ln\Lambda}.
\end{equation}
This is tidal capture, not three-body formation. Binary heating through
the existing $c_4$ terms is a separate process. The binary internal binding
energy is not an evolved state variable.

Let $v$ denote radial bulk velocity, $u$ random specific energy, and
$\epsilon=r_0^2/(u_0t_0^2)$. The conservative gas variables are
\begin{equation}
 W(\rho,v,u)=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\\rho v\\\rho(u+\epsilon v^2/2)\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
New binaries inherit the singles' bulk velocity and have random specific
energy $u_s/2$, as in the existing POWER\_LAW model. Freeze only the positive
per-donor frequency $k=m_b A\rho_s^n(u_s^n)^{-3/5}$ within a step. Then
\begin{align}
 F_s&=-kW_s,&
 F_b&=k\begin{pmatrix}\rho_s\\\rho_s v_s\\\rho_s(u_s/2+\epsilon v_s^2/2)\end{pmatrix},&F_d&=0.
\end{align}
These sources conserve local total mass and radial momentum. They remove
resolved random energy at rate $-k\rho_su_s/2$. This capture-cooling term
must not be described as numerical energy loss. Conservative recovery of
the binary velocity and energy also accounts for mixing streams of different
bulk velocity; merely updating binary $u$ would omit this contribution.

For cell volume $V_i=(r_{i+1/2}^3-r_{i-1/2}^3)/3$ and primitive increment
$\delta x_s=(\delta\rho_s,\delta v_s,\delta u_s)$, add
$-\Delta t V_i J_f$ to the single-star columns of each component's three
matrix rows, and add $\Delta t V_i F_f$ to its right-hand side. The Jacobians are
\begin{align}
 J_s&=-k\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0\\ v_s&\rho_s&0\\
 u_s+\epsilon v_s^2/2&\epsilon\rho_s v_s&\rho_s
 \end{pmatrix},\\
 J_b&=k\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0\\ v_s&\rho_s&0\\
 u_s/2+\epsilon v_s^2/2&\epsilon\rho_s v_s&\rho_s/2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
The additions lie within the existing $(13,14)$ half-bandwidth. They join
continuity, momentum, energy, and enclosed-mass constraints in the same solve;
there is no second evolution loop or post-solve density reset. Cost remains
$O(N)$. An isolated donor obeys
$\rho_s^{n+1}=\rho_s^n/(1+k\Delta t)$ while its $u_s$ and $v_s$ remain unchanged.
The frozen-frequency method is first-order consistent with the nonlinear
rate but is not identical to the hydrostatic solver's forward-Euler transfer
at finite timestep.

\section{Budgets and numerical checks}
The accepted-step ledgers use the same linearized sources as the matrix:
\begin{align}
 \Delta N_b&=\sum_i\frac{\Delta t V_i k_i}{m_b}(\rho_{s,i}+\delta\rho_{s,i}),\\
 \Delta E_{\mathrm{capture}}&=-\frac12\sum_i\Delta t V_i k_i
 (\rho_{s,i}u_{s,i}+u_{s,i}\delta\rho_{s,i}+\rho_{s,i}\delta u_{s,i}).
\end{align}
For example, binary mass plus integrated binary outflow minus
$m_bN_{\mathrm{formed}}$ stays constant. The gas-energy ledger separately
accounts for boundary energy flux, conduction, interaction heating, gravity
work, and capture cooling. It checks the discrete gas-energy equation, not
exact conservation of gas plus gravitational potential energy.

Capture can expose poor componentwise accuracy in almost unforced matrix
rows. We retain the original $10^{-8}$ backward-error threshold and refine
the original equations using the already computed band LU, with extended
precision residual accumulation. There are at most three correction solves,
so the asymptotic complexity is unchanged. Failed positivity or residual
checks terminate the run; there are no timestep retries.

The executable Mathematica companion is
\texttt{script/mathematica/moving\_capture.wls}. It derives every local matrix
addition and verifies mass, momentum, cooling, and the isolated-donor solution.
The C++ tests check species budgets through \texttt{evolve()}, nonzero-velocity
matrix entries, the gas-energy budget, parameter serialization, initialization,
and the public observer output.

\section{Statler comparison and limitations}
The target is the direct-heating-only calculation of Statler, Ostriker, and
Cohn (1987), Section IV b and Figs.~11--15. We use $N_*=3\times10^5$,
$M_*=0.7M_\odot$, $R_*=0.57R_\odot$, $r_0=1.13\,\mathrm{pc}$, and
$t_{rh}=225\,\mathrm{Myr}$. The initial model is an isotropic Plummer sphere,
with tiny nonzero binary and inert dark-component seeds. The radial domain
is $[0,10^4]$, with the first nonzero face at $10^{-6}$, matching the radial
range stated in the paper. The canonical hydrostatic initializer instead has
first face $10^{-2}$; this would not resolve an arbitrarily contracting core.
The analytic moving initializer uses $u=\rho_{\mathrm{tot},0}/(12\sqrt{1+r^2})$
and the existing discrete reference-force correction.

The plots use the existing public unit conversions:
$\rho_{\mathrm{Statler}}=3\rho/(4\pi)$,
$v_{\mathrm{rms,Statler}}^2=6u$, and unchanged radius $r/r_0$.
Time is converted using the initializer's physical $t_0/t_{rh}$, not fitted
to the collapse time. The heating-rate multiplier is $9t_{rh}/t_0$.
Scan overlays preserve the reference aspect ratios and axes. Profile legends
state the actual saved epochs. The selected targets $t/t_{rh}=18,50,350,2000$
are approximate square epochs read from Fig.~11a, not tabulated numbers from
the paper. The plotter requires a saved profile within 5\% of each target
and does not substitute a distant endpoint. This uncertainty matters for
quantitative profile fitting in Figs.~14 and 15.

The comparison matches initial scales, capture prescription, and displayed
observables, not the full kinetic model. HydroSim retains its local fluid
heating and conduction closures, whereas Statler solves orbit-averaged
Fokker--Planck equations and suppresses encounters that would eject stars in
the direct-heating-only case. No encounter-driven ejection or binary
disruption is added here. The fixed coefficient Coulomb logarithms do not
implement the paper's evolving core-number prescription.
Open fluid-boundary escape is separately measured;
it is not a substitute for an encounter-ejection prescription. Spatial and
timestep convergence must be checked before interpreting a residual mismatch.
\subsection{Completed runs and remaining discrepancy}
Both the 500- and 1000-cell runs reach $10^4t_{rh}$. The 500-cell run
peaks at $t/t_{rh}=14.3314$ with central density $42.5166$ in the plotted
units and ends with $3304.55$ binaries. The 1000-cell results are
$14.3257$, $42.9097$, and $3374.01$, respectively. For the 500-cell run,
mass including exported material changes by at most $1.18\times10^{-14}$;
the largest accepted-step gas-energy ledger residual is $4.58\times10^{-17}$.
Boundary export is only $1.44\times10^{-7}$ of the initial mass.

These checks do not establish a converged reproduction. The reference
central-density peak is several thousand rather than about 43, and
HydroSim expands its core much more strongly after the bounce. In addition,
the two resolutions disagree substantially at late times: the final
central binary-to-single density ratios are $0.0209$ and $0.0070$.
Time-aligned late density differences reach a factor of about ten.
Thus conservation and close agreement of the peak time and total binary
count do not validate the late core profiles. The overlays show this
failure rather than shifting or renormalizing curves to hide it.
Before assigning the discrepancy entirely to physical closures, the
moving solver needs spatial and timestep convergence of its post-collapse
core transport, reference-force correction, and energy/work quadrature.
